My Life Journey

Chapter OneA Child of Destiny

Every life has a beginning, but not every beginning is smooth. Some people are born into comfort and stability, while others enter the world amidst trials and uncertainties. My own story belongs to the latter, yet it is a testimony that God's purpose cannot be hindered by difficult circumstances.

I was born into a family whose foundation was shaken by religious differences. Although my parents genuinely loved each other, their marriage became a battlefield of conflicting beliefs. My father, Lawrence Olawale Odusina, was a devoted Christian whose faith in Jesus Christ shaped every aspect of his life. He believed that his home should be built upon biblical principles and that his family should serve the Lord.

On the other hand, my maternal grandmother remained deeply committed to traditional religion. She believed that her daughter should uphold the customs and religious practices of her ancestors.

This disagreement eventually grew beyond a mere difference of opinion and became a family crisis.

It was in the midst of this conflict that I was born. Instead of my arrival bringing lasting peace to the family, the disagreements became more intense. My maternal grandmother insisted that her daughter abandon Christianity and embrace traditional worship, while my father firmly refused to compromise his Christian convictions.

When no agreement could be reached, my parents separated. My mother returned to her family, while six months after my birth, my father took me into his custody. Thus, from infancy, my life was marked by separation from my mother—a painful reality that would shape many of my early experiences.

Although I was too young to understand what had happened, I later realized that God's unseen hand was guiding my destiny. Human beings may make decisions that alter the course of a family, but they cannot cancel God's eternal purpose. As the Scriptures declare, "Many are the plans in a person's heart, but it is the Lord's purpose that prevails"

(Proverbs 19:21).

Growing up without the daily presence of my mother was not easy. There were moments when questions filled my mind, and I wondered why my life had begun differently from that of many other children. Yet, with the passage of time, I came to understand that God often prepares His servants through unusual circumstances. Many great men and women in the Bible experienced rejection, hardship, or separation before fulfilling their divine assignments.

My father became more than a parent; he was my guide, protector, and early teacher. He laboured diligently to provide for me and to ensure that I was raised in the fear of God. Through his example, I learned the importance of prayer, honesty, discipline, and unwavering faith in Christ. His steadfast commitment to Christianity laid the spiritual foundation upon which my own life would later be built.

Looking back over those early years, I can confidently say that every challenge became a stepping stone rather than a stumbling block. What seemed like misfortune was, in reality, God's preparation for a greater purpose.

My childhood taught me resilience, dependence upon God, and the conviction that divine destiny is stronger than human circumstances.

As I reflect on those formative years, I see the faithfulness of God at every stage.

He preserved my life, guided my steps, and continually demonstrated that His plans are always higher than our own. My story is therefore not merely a record of family struggles but a testimony of divine grace, providence, and the triumph of God's purpose over adversity.

This was only the beginning of a remarkable journey—a journey through childhood, education, spiritual discovery, and eventually the call into Christian ministry. Every chapter that follows reveals another evidence of God's unfailing hand upon my life.

My Childhood: A Battle for Survival

My childhood was far from ordinary. Like many children who grow up without the love and daily presence of a mother, my early years were filled with uncertainty and constant adjustment. From infancy, I was moved from one relative to another within my father's family. Those years were not easy; they were, in many respects, a battle for survival.

Despite these challenges, God surrounded me with family members who loved and cared for me. My paternal grandmother, affectionately known as Iya Ologiri, played a remarkable role in my upbringing. She sacrificed greatly to ensure that I was cared for and never lacked the affection that a grandmother could give.

My grandfather, Pa Simeon Odusina, also contributed immensely to my welfare. His wisdom, guidance, and concern for my future left a lasting impression on my early life. In addition, my father's siblings lovingly shared the responsibility of raising me. Among them were Mrs. Adetona (popularly known as Mama Ade) and Mama Taiwo (Iya Joko). They welcomed me into their homes, cared for me as their own child, and provided the love and support that helped me through those difficult years.

Although I experienced the pain of growing up without my mother's daily care, I was never completely abandoned. God used these members of my father's family as instruments of His grace and provision. Their sacrifices and unwavering commitment ensured that I survived those challenging years and laid the foundation for the person I would later become.

I remained under the loving care of my grandparents and other relatives until I was about four years old. At that stage, a new chapter of my life began when I finally went to live with my father. Living with him marked the beginning of a closer father-and-son relationship and introduced me to new experiences that would further shape my character, faith, and future.

Early Stage of My Education

My father placed great value on education despite the many challenges he faced as a railway worker. His deep love for me was demonstrated by his determination to ensure that I received a good education. When I reached the age of six, he brought me back from Kutiwenji, a Nupe railway station in what is now Niger State, where I had been staying with relatives, so that I could begin my formal education.

Because of the nature of his work as a railway employee, he was liable to be transferred from one station to another at any time. To provide me with a stable beginning, he returned me to our hometown, Imodi, where I was enrolled at St. Matthew's Primary School, Imodi, in 1966. My education began on a promising note, and by 1967 I had progressed to Primary Two.

In 1967, my father was transferred back to the southern part of the country, specifically to Olokemeji. As a result, I joined him there and continued my primary education at Methodist Primary School, Olokemeji, where I studied from 1968 to 1971. Those years provided me with a solid educational foundation, although the frequent relocations caused by my father's employment meant that my schooling was often interrupted.

As I prepared for secondary education, my father sought a more permanent arrangement. After consulting one of his superiors, the Station Master Mr Koyi, whose father was the headmaster of a school in Odosenbora, he arranged for me to live with the family so that I could continue my education in a stable environment. Consequently, I was admitted to Christ Church Primary School, Odosenbora, where I completed my primary education between 1971 and 1972.

Upon completing primary school, I gained admission into Ijebu Southern District Grammar School, Ala, where I attended from 1973 to 1976. Unfortunately, this period marked one of the most difficult chapters of my educational journey. Living alone in a rented apartment without parental supervision exposed me to numerous challenges. The absence of proper guidance, discipline, and emotional support negatively affected both my academic performance and personal development.

Despite repeated efforts, I was unable to progress to Form Four after three years. Eventually, I was advised to withdraw from the school.

My father believed that my academic struggles were largely due to the lack of parental care and supervision. Determined to help me recover academically, he entrusted me to one of his close friends, Mr. Balogun, in Ibadan. Sadly, this arrangement did not produce the desired results. My guardian was unable to secure admission for me into a recognized secondary school, and while searching for opportunities, I unknowingly became enrolled in an unapproved private school Iwajowa comprehensive high school Omowumi Orita Aperin Ibadan.

The situation became even more disappointing when the Oyo State Ministry of Education closed the school for failing to meet the required standards.

To reassure students and parents, the proprietor claimed that we had been registered to sit for the West African School Certificate Examination through a school in Offa. We initially assumed he meant Offa in Kwara State, but we later discovered that he was referring to Offa-Igbo in Lagelu Local Government Area of Oyo State.

In 1978, we were transferred to Offa-Igbo with the expectation that we would eventually sit for the West African Examinations Council (WAEC) examination. Unfortunately, this promise never materialized. Travelling daily from Ibadan to Offa-Igbo became both physically exhausting and financially burdensome. In an effort to reduce the hardship, my father arranged for me to stay with another of his friends at Lalupon Railway Station, which was much closer to Offa-Igbo. When that also proved ineffective, I was moved into the school's boarding house. Despite these sacrifices, my educational aspirations remained unfulfilled.

A turning point came when my father was transferred to Offa Railway Station. Consequently, I was withdrawn from Iwajowa Comprehensive High School, Offa-Igbo, and moved to live with him. Although living with my father restored the warmth of parental care, there was no immediate opportunity for me to continue my education. Faced with this difficult reality, I began engaging in manual labour to support myself, taking on jobs such as working as a motor boy and other forms of casual labour.

During this period as a victim of a broken marriage the home became umberable , I decided to run away from home to go back to Ibadan to go and join motor boy, but there came a divine intervention, I decided to notify a member of our family friend ,Nike Ojo, who later became my lovely wife, when I informed her of my plan she began to weep so I decided to suspend my plan till another time but I will not inform her again that was Saturday, luckily for me my admission letter to Ojoku grammar school came on Monday another rays of hope emerged

Although these years were marked by disappointment, hardship, and interrupted education, they also became a period of resilience and character formation. The trials I experienced taught me perseverance, patience, humility, and complete dependence on God. Looking back today, I recognize that what appeared to be setbacks were, in fact, stepping stones that prepared me for the greater purpose God had designed for my life.

The Final Stage of My Secondary School Education

The final stage of my secondary school education became yet another remarkable chapter in my life's journey. After staying at home for almost a year, I returned to school filled with joy, hope, and renewed determination. I was admitted into Ojoku Grammar School during the second term of Form Four because the principal insisted that I could not be admitted directly into Form Five.

Thus, my educational journey continued. The late Mr. Lawanson, the principal, took a special interest in my academic progress. He constantly encouraged me and arranged extra lessons to help me recover from the academic setback caused by my long absence from school. His support inspired me greatly. At the same time, I resolved within myself to work hard and finish my education successfully.

At that time, the promotion policy at Ojoku Grammar School was very strict. Every student was required to score at least fifty percent in every subject before being promoted to the next class. Despite my hard work, another obstacle emerged. One of my teachers awarded me 49.9% in Yoruba, making me fall just short of the required pass mark. Consequently, I was informed that I would have to repeat Form Four.

Although disappointed,

I refused to be discouraged.

I remained hopeful and continued to believe that my efforts would eventually be rewarded. After several weeks of discussions and consultations among the teachers and the principal, the decision was reviewed, and I was finally permitted to join my classmates in Form Five.

Soon afterward, Principal Lawanson was transferred, and a new principal assumed office. His arrival almost reopened another painful chapter in my educational journey. The 1979–1980 academic session coincided with a period of intense political rivalry in Nigeria between the National Party of Nigeria (NPN) and the Unity Party of Nigeria (UPN). The principal and vice principal were known supporters of the NPN, while twelve of the thirteen students in my class were from the western part of the country. I was the only student from Ogun State, specifically Imodi, Ijebu, the home region associated with Chief Obafemi Awolowo, the presidential candidate of the UPN.

Political sentiments unfortunately found their way into the school environment, creating tension and suspicion. At one point, it was even suggested that I would not be allowed to complete my education in the school and that I should return to my home state. Rather than allowing fear or bitterness to overwhelm me,

I remained calm, cautious, and determined to achieve my goal.

By the grace of God and through perseverance,

I overcame every obstacle. In June and July 1980,

I successfully sat for the West African School Certificate Examination (WASC), bringing to a close one of the most challenging yet rewarding chapters of my secondary school education.

Another Phase of My Life

Life is made up of different phases, and each phase brings its own opportunities, challenges, and lessons. Immediately after I completed my secondary school education in June 1980, my hope of furthering my education seemed very slim. Having considered all that I had gone through to complete secondary school, coupled with the heavy financial responsibilities my father was already carrying for the family, I knew that pursuing higher education immediately would be very difficult. Nevertheless, I refused to lose hope.

I resolved to seek employment so that I could support myself and finance my future education. My ambition was to work in a bank, so I contacted my friend in Ibadan, Brother Gbenga Afolabi, who helped arrange an interview for me at the former National Bank on 10 October 1980.

Unknown to me, my father had also been making efforts to secure a job for me with the Nigerian Railway Corporation. Coincidentally, I was invited for an interview by the Railway Corporation on the very same day and at the same time as the bank interview. Faced with this difficult decision, I chose to honour and obey my father's wish by attending the railway interview and giving up my desire to work in the banking sector.

That decision marked the beginning of another significant chapter in my life. I was offered employment by the Nigerian Railway Corporation and was immediately sent for six months of training at the Traffic Training School, Ebute Metta, Lagos.

During the training, I met one of my town's sons, Mr. Adedayo Mosuro. We became close friends, and our friendship would later play an important role in my life's journey, which I shall discuss in a later chapter.

After successfully completing both the training and practical sessions, I was posted to Biribiri Station, near Bacita Junction along the Jebba Road, for my first official assignment with the Nigerian Railway Corporation. My experience there became another school of life, where I learned valuable lessons in endurance, patience, humility, and perseverance while working under a very demanding supervisor.

In 1982, I was transferred to Ilorin. Despite the challenges of my job, I remained determined to pursue higher education. During this period, I prepared for and resat both the General Certificate of Education (GCE) Ordinary Level and Advanced Level examinations in anticipation of gaining admission for further studies.

While serving in Ilorin, I encountered several difficulties with my immediate superior. The working environment became increasingly challenging, couple with the challenges I was having in my relationship with my girlfriend and after careful consideration, I requested a transfer. My request was eventually approved, and in 1984, I was transferred to Jebba, where another phase of my life's journey began.

Life at Jebba

Every stage of life has its own unique experiences, lessons, and turning points. My arrival at Jebba marked another significant phase in my life. Having gone through the challenges and disappointments I experienced in Ilorin,

I decided to become more reserved and focused, determined to face life with greater seriousness and responsibility.

By this time, my hope of furthering my education had almost disappeared. Despite the efforts I had made to continue my studies, every available opportunity seemed to close before me. I even considered correspondence courses as an alternative, but those efforts also failed. I tried several forms of business and vocational work in the hope of improving my income and creating a better future for myself, but none of these attempts produced the results I desired.

It was during this difficult period that I began to hear what I perceived as the voice of God directing my life. I sensed that God was telling me that the path I was pursuing was not the way He intended to lead me. At that time, however, I could not fully understand or comprehend what God was saying to me. Looking back today, I can see that God was gradually preparing me for a different purpose and direction.

A New Spiritual Beginning

As I have mentioned earlier, my life at Jebba brought a new turn to my journey. One person who played an important role in this transformation was my friend, Bode Olaifa. He encouraged me to start attending church regularly, and I accepted his advice.

My decision to attend church became the beginning of a deeper relationship with God. I gradually moved closer to God and became actively involved in the activities of the Cherubim and Seraphim Movement Church, Sabo, Jebba. I joined the choir and served diligently. At different times, I served as the choir secretary and assistant, working closely with Mrs. Yetunde Olagunju.

Our responsibilities in the choir brought us into close contact and eventually developed into a strong family friendship. Through the church, I learned the importance of commitment, service, discipline, and fellowship. My involvement in the church also gave me the opportunity to meet many people from different backgrounds and to establish meaningful relationships within the community.

Life at the Railway Corporation

My employment with the Railway Corporation at Jebba Station also contributed significantly to my development. My work at the railway brought me into contact with many people and exposed me to different aspects of society. Through both my employment and my involvement in the church, I gradually became known and respected by many people in the community.

Although I had not been able to continue my formal education or establish myself through vocational training, I began to discover that life had other dimensions of development, with encounter with my friends at Jebba Rotimi Aluko aka Eri now late and Prophet Oluwatosin God was using my circumstances, relationships, work, and church service to shape my character and prepare me for what lay ahead.

The Desire to Settle Down

After making several unsuccessful attempts to further my education and acquire vocational skills, I began to think seriously about marriage. I felt that it was time to settle down, build a family, and begin planning for the future.

However, finding the right person was not easy. I made efforts to meet a suitable lady, both within the community and through the church, but these efforts did not yield the desired result. The more I tried, the more I realized that this was not something I could accomplish by human effort alone.

This experience brought me even closer to God. I began to seek His face more earnestly, asking Him to guide me and direct my steps. I did not want to enter into marriage merely because I was eager to settle down. I wanted to make the right decision and build a family that would be different from some of the difficult experiences I had encountered in my own childhood and upbringing.

I had experienced hardship and uncertainty in life, and I did not want my future children to go through the same experiences. I therefore became more prayerful, trusting God to provide the right partner and to guide me into the future He had prepared for me.

Looking Back

Today, when I look back at my years in Jebba, I realize that what appeared at the time to be a period of disappointment was actually a period of divine preparation. My inability to continue my education, the failure of my vocational efforts, my work at the railway, my involvement in the church, and my search for a life partner were all contributing to the person I was gradually becoming.

Jebba was more than just another place where I lived and worked. It became a place where my spiritual life began to take a deeper shape. It was there that I began to understand that life is not always determined by our plans and expectations. Sometimes, closed doors can become instruments through which God redirects our steps.

Although I could not fully understand God's plan for my life at that time, I continued moving forward. Little did I know that the experiences I was having at Jebba were preparing me for another major phase of my life.

Angelic Interventions

The road to greatness and success is rarely smooth. It is often filled with obstacles, disappointments, dangers, and unexpected challenges. However, in the midst of all these experiences, God does not abandon His people. Looking back over my life, I can confidently testify to the kindness, mercy, protection, and faithfulness of God.

At this point in my life story, I feel compelled to acknowledge the many occasions when God intervened in situations that could have ended differently. There were moments when I came dangerously close to disaster, yet somehow, a way of escape always appeared. I have come to describe these experiences as angelic interventions—those moments when divine protection seemed to come just when I needed it most.

There is an old song that I remember from my childhood which says, in essence:

“When it remains only a little time for a child of a good heart to fall into a pit, there will be a divine light to show him the way of escape.”

That statement describes many experiences in my life. Whenever danger appeared to be waiting ahead of me, somehow a light seemed to shine, revealing the danger and directing me toward another path.

Protection During My Childhood

During my tender years, I grew up without adequate maternal care and attention. As a young child, I was often left to navigate life with limited guidance and supervision. Naturally, a child in such circumstances could easily become vulnerable to many dangers.

Yet, when I look back, I realize that I was never completely alone. On several occasions, I came close to situations that could have brought serious consequences, but God always seemed to provide a way of escape.

One particular incident happened in my hometown.

There was a traditional festival known as Oro, a cultural practice that was surrounded by restrictions and customs, including the prohibition against women seeing the procession. As a child, I was naturally curious and wanted to know what was happening whenever the festival was being observed.

One night, the Oro procession was taking place directly in front of our house. Because of my curiosity, I quietly sneaked out of the house without my grandfather's knowledge and sat at the doorstep, hoping to see what was happening.

I had hardly settled down when one of the people in the procession noticed me. He challenged whoever was sitting there. At that moment, fear immediately took hold of me. I quickly ran inside the house, locked the door, and remained there.

The following morning, the people involved told my grandfather what I had done. It was a frightening experience, but I thank God that I was a boy and that the circumstances did not result in anything worse.

Looking back today, I see that incident as another example of God's protection over my life. My childhood curiosity could have exposed me to serious danger, but God provided a way of escape.

God's Provision During My Difficult Days at School

Another significant experience occurred during my secondary school days at Ijebu Southern District Grammar School, Ala, where I lived at Idowa.

At that time, my father was living far away, and I was given only a small amount of money each month to cover my feeding and accommodation. Life was extremely difficult. The money I received was expected to take care of me for the entire month, but because of the difficult circumstances, it was sometimes exhausted in less than two weeks.

There were moments when I wondered how I would survive until the next month.

But God, in His infinite mercy, provided help in an unexpected way.

It reminds me of the biblical story of the prophet Elijah, whom God sustained through an unusual means of provision. In my own case, God used two young teenagers, Taye and Kehinde Gafari, as instruments of His provision.

Their father was an herbalist, and whenever animals such as goats, fowls, or ducks were slaughtered, the two boys would bring some of the meat to me. They would also bring garri, and these became the means by which I survived until the time when my father would send money again.

What might appear to be an ordinary act of kindness to another person was, to me, a divine provision. At a time when I had no clear means of sustaining myself, God raised people who became instruments of His mercy.

That experience taught me an important lesson: God can use anybody, anywhere, and at any time to meet the needs of His children.

The Agemo Experience

Another unforgettable experience occurred during my secondary school days at Ala.

There was an annual traditional festival known as the Agemo Festival, celebrated at Imosan, a border town close to my hometown, Imodi. During the festival, adherents would travel to Imosan every year. One notable aspect of the journey was that they did not normally travel by vehicle; they trekked, carrying their belongings with them.

There were also traditional restrictions surrounding their movement, particularly concerning women seeing them during their journey. As a result, considerable awareness was usually created whenever they were about to pass through an area so that people could observe the restrictions.

As a young boy, I had heard many stories about the Agemo and the supernatural acts associated with the festival. My curiosity was aroused, and I wanted to see for myself what happened.

One particular year,

I decided to leave school around 10:00 a.m. so that I could follow them and witness what I imagined would be extraordinary events.

I had carefully planned everything in my mind. I assumed that when they eventually reached Imosan and entered their grove, I would simply cross over to my hometown, which was not very far away. I also thought that if darkness began to approach, I could move ahead of them, find a vehicle, and return home.

Unfortunately, in the excitement and enthusiasm of the moment, I forgot one very important thing: money.

I left the money I had on my table at school.

The journey continued, but to my disappointment, I did not see any of the magical or spectacular events I had imagined. By about 4:00 p.m., hunger began to take its toll on me. I wanted to move ahead and buy something to eat, but that was when the reality of my situation suddenly became clear.

I had no money.

There was no easy way to return.

The distance between where I was and home was considerable, and night was already approaching.

At that point, I realized that my only option was to continue walking.

So I began the long journey home on foot.

The journey continued through the night. I passed through the direction of Ijebu-Ode and continued toward Imodi. It was a long, exhausting, and frightening journey for a young boy.

Eventually, at about 1:30 a.m., I arrived safely at home.

When I look back at that experience today, I can only say: God was with me.

A young boy leaving school in the morning without informing anyone, following a traditional procession for hours, losing track of time, running out of food and money, and eventually trekking through the night could easily have encountered serious danger.

Yet I arrived home safely.

God, My Shield and Protector

These experiences are only a few of the many situations in which I believe God intervened in my life. From my childhood to my school days and through the different stages of my life, I have experienced situations where danger seemed very close, yet somehow a way of escape appeared.

I cannot explain every circumstance simply in human terms. When I look back, I see the hand of God.

The Scripture says:

“For he shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways.” —

Psalm 91:11

That Scripture has become meaningful to me because I have experienced God's protection in ways that I could not have arranged for myself.

I have learned that God's protection does not always mean that we will never face danger. Sometimes, it means that we will pass through the danger and come out safely. At other times, it means that God will reveal the danger before it happens and provide another route.

My life has not been without difficulties. I have experienced deprivation, uncertainty, disappointment, loneliness, and circumstances that could easily have discouraged me. Yet through them all, God has remained faithful.

God has been my shield.

God has been my provider.

God has been my guide.

God has been my protector.

As I reflect on these experiences, I can confidently say that my survival was not merely the result of human strength or wisdom. There were moments when divine mercy spoke on my behalf.

Indeed, God was on my side, and He remains my shield.

The Danger of Growing Up Without Adequate Parental Care

Parental care is very essential in the life of every child. The presence, guidance, protection, and supervision of parents can make a great difference in the life of a growing child. Looking back at my childhood, I cannot overemphasize the importance of this reality. Some of the experiences I went through were dangerous situations that could easily have ended my life. Yet, in the midst of those circumstances, I experienced what I can only describe as divine and angelic interventions.

One particular incident happened during my days at Ijebu Southern District Grammar School, Ala, while I was living at Idowa. There was a large plantation of eucalyptus trees along the road leading to my school and around the area where I lived. The trees were very tall and, to a young boy like me, they were fascinating. My youthful inquisitiveness often made me want to explore things without fully understanding the dangers involved.

One day, there was a heavy storm that caused some of the large eucalyptus trees to fall. After the storm had passed, I became curious and decided to go and examine the fallen trees. I called some of the younger boys to come along with me. When we got there, I decided to climb one of the trees while the other boys stood below and watched.

As I climbed higher and higher, I became excited by the adventure. Unfortunately, I did not realize how dangerous the situation had become. When I reached the top, the branch on which I was standing suddenly tore away. In an instant, I lost my balance. Every effort I made to regain my footing proved unsuccessful, and I fell heavily onto the fallen trees beneath me.

As I fell, other branches also came crashing down on me. At that moment, I was convinced that I had died. I could not move properly. I had sustained serious injuries to my back, and my mouth and tongue were badly injured. Even today, I still have a visible mark on my tongue from that accident, and whenever I accidentally overstretch my back, I am reminded of the pain and injury I suffered on that day.

What happened next remains one of the clearest reminders to me of God's mercy. Immediately after the accident, the other boys ran away in fear. I was left lying helplessly among the fallen trees. For all practical purposes, I appeared to be dead. There was no parent around, no adult to supervise what had happened, and no immediate medical assistance available.

But God did not allow my life to end there.

After some time, I opened my eyes. I saw one of the boys who had remained nearby, watching from a distance. With the little strength I had left, I signaled to him for help. He immediately called the other boys, and they returned to rescue me.

Because of the injuries to my back, mouth, and tongue, I could not lie comfortably on my body. The boys had to drag me home. I was eventually laid on a mat, where I remained for almost three weeks. There was no hospital treatment or proper medical attention. I survived largely through what could be described as self-medication and God's sustaining grace.

Looking back now, I see that experience differently. At the time, I was only a young boy who had gone out of curiosity and adventure. I did not understand the seriousness of the danger I was exposing myself to. But as an adult reflecting on my childhood, I have come to appreciate how vulnerable a child can be when adequate parental care and supervision are absent.

One of the painful aspects of this particular incident is that neither of my parents ever knew what had happened to me. I carried the memory and the scars of that experience throughout my life, while my parents eventually passed away without knowing that their son had once come so close to death.

This is one of the reasons I strongly believe that children need more than food, clothing, and education. They need presence, supervision, guidance, protection, affection, and parental attention. Sending a child to a distant school or allowing a child to live far away from home without adequate adult supervision can expose that child to dangers that parents may never know about.

My experience at Idowa was not simply an accident; it became a testimony. What could have been the end of my life became another chapter in my story. I believe that the hand of God preserved me when there was no human hand available to rescue me. The boy who remained behind, saw me, responded to my signal, and called others for help became an instrument through which God intervened.

When I look back at that incident, I cannot explain my survival merely by coincidence. I see the mercy of God and what I call an angelic intervention. God preserved my life even when I was too young to understand the value of the life He had given me.

That accident left physical marks on my body, but it also left a deeper spiritual impression on my heart. It taught me that there are moments in life when human beings may be helpless, but God can still make a way. It strengthened my conviction that my life has been preserved through many dangers for a purpose.

Indeed, the journey of my life has been filled with moments when I could have been lost, but somehow, God made a way of escape. The incident at Idowa was one of those moments. I fell expecting death, but I woke up to life. I was abandoned among the fallen trees, but God provided someone who could see me. I was injured and helpless, but help eventually came.

That is why I call these experiences ANGELIC INTERVENTIONS—not because I saw angels physically, but because, in moments when human help seemed unavailable, God mysteriously provided a way for me to survive.

My story is therefore not only about the dangers I encountered as a child. It is also a story of divine preservation, God's unfailing mercy, and the mysterious ways through which He protects a person whose journey is not yet complete..

In Continuation

God, in His infinite love and mercy, has never abandoned His own without care. Throughout the journey of life, especially during difficult and uncertain seasons, He often places people in our paths who become instruments of His protection, encouragement, provision, and guidance. Looking back at my own life, I cannot but remember the story of Joseph in the Bible. Joseph went through many difficult stages on the road to the fulfilment of his destiny, yet God was with him throughout. At different points in his life, God used people to preserve him from destruction and to move him closer to the purpose for which he was created.

In the same way, as I reflect on the journey of my own life, I will at this point acknowledge some of the people whom God graciously placed along my path as angels in human form. Some appeared during moments of loneliness, some during seasons of uncertainty, while others came at critical turning points when their intervention became indispensable to my survival, progress, and destiny.

Brother Gbenga Afolabi

Brother Gbenga Afolabi, as I fondly called him, came into my life at a time when I was practically walking alone in Ibadan. I met him during the period when I was seeking admission to continue my secondary education.

Our first meeting took place at Elekuro Grammar School. Interestingly, he and his brother were also seeking admission at that time. We were both directed to Iwajowa Comprehensive High School at Omowumi area, near Orita Aperin, Ibadan. It was there that our friendship began.

Gbenga became a very important person during that period of the second phase of my secondary school education. His family opened their hearts and home to me. Many weekends, we would leave school and go to their house in Ibadan, and gradually, I became more than just a friend; I became a member of their family.

Looking back today, I realize that our friendship was not accidental. At a time when I needed companionship, acceptance, and a sense of belonging, God brought Gbenga into my life. Through him and his family, I experienced a kind of family relationship that I deeply cherished.

Our friendship has endured through the years, and till today, we are still friends. Whenever I remember those difficult days of my educational journey, I remember Gbenga with gratitude. He was indeed one of the angels God placed on my path.

Osunkeye Olayiwola

During the final stage of my secondary school education, another angel had already been prepared for me at Ojoku Grammar School. His name was Osunkeye Olayiwola, though he is now late. May his soul continue to rest in perfect peace.

At Ojoku Grammar School, Olayiwola and I became friends. As time went on, we made a remarkable discovery: we were both living on the same street, and, interestingly, our fathers were both railway staff. These similarities created a natural bond between us, but our relationship eventually went far beyond ordinary friendship.

Our friendship became so deep that our two families became family friends. We shared experiences, challenges, hopes, and aspirations. He played a significant role in my life during that period, a role that I can never forget.

Interestingly, while God used him significantly in my life, God also used me in a meaningful way in his family. This is one of the beautiful mysteries of human relationships. Sometimes, we may think that someone has been sent into our lives only to help us, but God may also be using us to fulfil a purpose in that person's life.

When I look back, I see clearly that our relationship was part of God's divine arrangement. Olayiwola was not merely a schoolmate or neighbour; he was one of those people whom God used to make my journey easier.

Fatumbi Kayode and His Family

Another person I must acknowledge is Fatumbi Kayode and his family.

I met Kayode at the Cherubim and Seraphim Movement Church in Jebba, where we both served as choir members. Our friendship became stronger during the early years of my marriage, particularly around 1987 and 1988.

His wife also played a significant role in the early years of my marriage. She became a counsellor and source of guidance to my wife. Her counsel and support were valuable, especially during those early years when my wife and I were learning to build our home together.

Later, our paths crossed again at Offa during one of the difficult periods of my life. Once again, Kayode and his family demonstrated genuine love and support. They stood by us when we needed people who could understand our situation and offer meaningful assistance.

When I reflect on their contribution to my journey, I see another example of God's faithfulness. God does not always send angels in supernatural forms. Sometimes, He sends them as friends, families, colleagues, neighbours, church members, or even people we meet unexpectedly.

Adebayo Mosuro, a Friend God Sent for a Special Purpose

Before I close this section, there are still many other people whom God used at different stages of my life. Their stories will be mentioned as this work progresses. However, there is one person whose contribution to my life is particularly precious to me, and I must give him special attention here.

His name is Adebayo Mosuro, whom I fondly call Bayo.

As I stated earlier, Bayo and I met at the Railway Traffic Training School, Ebute Metta, Lagos. At the time we met, neither of us knew the extent to which our friendship would develop or the special purpose for which God was bringing us together.

We became friends during our training days. After completing our training, we were posted to different railway stations. Physically, we were separated, but distance did not weaken the bond between us. Instead, our friendship became even stronger.

Bayo and his family lives in Ibadan, and whenever I travelled to Ibadan, I would often spend as much as a week with his family. His family received me with so much love and warmth that people sometimes assumed I was a member of the family.

Even when Bayo himself was not around, his mother would give me the key to his room and allow me to stay with them. I was adequately cared for and treated with genuine affection. I never felt like a stranger in their home.

That kind of acceptance is difficult to forget.

Our friendship reminds me, in some measure, of the friendship between David and Jonathan in the Bible. There was a deep bond of love, loyalty, trust, and genuine concern between us.

There were times when Bayo would travel all the way from Ibadan to Jebba simply because he wanted to see me. He would sometimes stay for less than two hours and then return to Ibadan.

On one particular occasion, he came and simply said:

“Dare, I just felt like seeing you.”

And after spending a short time with me, he would return to Ibadan.

I must emphasize that this was during the era when there were no mobile phones. There was no WhatsApp, no text message, and no instant communication. If someone wanted to see you, the person had to make the physical journey. Therefore, for Bayo to travel such a long distance simply because he wanted to see me was a demonstration of a friendship that went beyond convenience.

Bayo and the Divinely Arranged Wedding

Another very significant role God used Bayo to play in my life was during my wedding.

As I have stated earlier, my wedding was not something we had carefully planned from the beginning. It was, in many ways, a divinely arranged wedding.

Circumstances arose between the two families, and my father had to make a rather hasty decision concerning our marriage. I have already mentioned the deep love my father had for me, even though he was not always able to express that love openly.

Let me also state something important here: my father and my in-laws had been friends from their youthful days. They had lived together, married around the same period, and, in many respects, had lived almost like one family.

Therefore, when the circumstances surrounding our relationship became known to my father, he quickly resolved that we should get married. His concern was that nothing should happen that would destroy the long-standing friendship between the two families.

That was how we suddenly found ourselves planning a wedding that we had not originally scheduled.

It was at this critical point that Bayo demonstrated another dimension of his friendship.

He practically shouldered the financial burden of the wedding. At a time when the wedding had come upon us unexpectedly and we had not made adequate preparations, Bayo stepped forward and provided the support we desperately needed.

His intervention was not merely financial. It was an expression of friendship, sacrifice, responsibility, and love.

His support did not end with the wedding. Even after we got married and our first child, Oredolapo, was born, Bayo continued to stand by us.

He would buy cartons of baby food for my daughter and provide other necessary things for the baby. He continued to support the family in ways that went beyond what one would normally expect from an ordinary friend.

When I look back at those years, I can confidently say that Bayo was more than a friend to me. He was God's provision at a critical moment in my life.

God used him to fill gaps that I could not fill by myself. He appeared at moments when I needed help, and his presence brought relief when circumstances seemed overwhelming.

This is why I describe people like Bayo as angels in human form.

They may not have wings, and they may not come with supernatural appearances, but their actions carry the fingerprints of divine intervention. They arrive at the right time, say the right words, provide the needed assistance, and sometimes make sacrifices that change the course of another person's life.

As I continue to reflect on my journey, I realize that I have not travelled alone. There were moments when I thought I was walking by myself, but God had already placed people ahead of me. Some opened doors, some provided shelter, some gave encouragement, some offered financial assistance, some gave wise counsel, and others simply stayed when they could have walked away.

Today, I give God all the glory for every one of them.

I may not be able to mention all their names at this point, but their contributions are permanently written in the story of my life. As this work progresses, I shall continue to mention some of these people and the different ways God used them.

Indeed, when God decides to preserve a destiny, He often sends people before us who become His hands, His voice, His provision, and His protection.

For this reason, I remain eternally grateful to God for the many angels in human form He placed along the journey of my life.

My Service Years

Service to one’s nation is a privilege that demands commitment, dedication, discipline, and loyalty. As I stated earlier, I joined the Nigerian Railway Corporation in November 1980, immediately after completing my secondary school education. I saw the opportunity as a stepping stone toward achieving my academic ambitions while also contributing my quota to the development of my nation.

Shortly after my appointment, I was sent to the Traffic Training School, Ebute Meta, Lagos, where I underwent six months of intensive training. Upon completion of the training, I was posted to Abeokuta for one month of practical experience. Thereafter, in 1981, I was posted to Biribiri Station, near Bacita Junction along the Jebba Road in Kwara State. This marked the beginning of my practical journey with the Nigerian Railway Corporation.

At Biribiri Station, I was still a young and inexperienced officer. I encountered a very strict senior officer who paid close attention to every detail of my work and carefully recorded even my minor mistakes. Initially, this experience was intimidating, but it taught me an important lesson: I had to be disciplined, careful, and upright in the discharge of my responsibilities.

As a result, I decided to study the Railway General Rules and Instructions in depth. I also studied the Manual of Accounts and other relevant regulations governing railway operations. This decision became one of the most important steps I took during my service years. The knowledge I acquired through those studies proved invaluable throughout my career and helped me to carry out my duties with greater confidence and understanding.

As time went on, I had the privilege of working with other senior officers, including Mr. Oke and Mr. Bayeri. We developed a good working relationship, and I enjoyed the companionship and cooperation that existed among us.

God's Protection in the Midst of Danger

Looking back on my years of service, I cannot tell the story without acknowledging the faithfulness and protection of God. Railway work was not without its hazards, particularly for those of us who worked night duties. There were occasions when I encountered situations that could easily have resulted in tragedy, but God continually preserved my life.

As a Station Master, part of my schedule involved night duty, usually from 6:00 p.m. to 6:00 a.m. One particular night, at about 1:00 a.m., we were expecting an express train. I therefore had to remain extremely vigilant at the railway point—the location where the movement of trains along the railway line was controlled.

The point was situated near a level crossing leading to the station, an area frequently used by Fulani herdsmen as they moved their cattle. Because of this, we had to be particularly watchful, especially at night.

While I was standing in the darkness and watching the area in front of the station, I suddenly noticed some figures moving across the light indicator as they approached the station. At first, I thought they might be thieves or people with criminal intentions. My curiosity got the better of me, and I wanted to see their faces clearly.

To my surprise, I saw three men dressed completely in white agbada, with matching shoes and caps. Their appearance was unusual and their movement was mysterious. I quickly hid myself behind one of the pillars outside the station, hoping that the approaching train would illuminate their faces and enable me to identify them.

But as the rays of light from the approaching train became brighter, something extraordinary happened—the three men simply vanished.

I could not explain what I had seen. Whether they were human beings or something beyond human understanding, I leave that matter to God. What I know is that the experience left a deep impression on me and reminded me that there are dangers and mysteries in life that human beings cannot always explain. I became even more conscious of the need to trust God for protection.

On another occasion, I encountered python snakes on the railway line during the night. Such encounters were frightening, especially when working in darkness and in isolated locations. Yet, through all these experiences, God remained faithful. I can look back today and say with confidence that the hand of God was upon my life throughout my years of railway service.

My Years in Ilorin

In 1982, I was transferred to Ilorin. There, I was reunited with some of my colleagues from the training school, including Akintunde Adeniran, Oyeyemi Raufu (now late), Victor Folarin, and several others.

My years of service in Ilorin became a significant foundation for the next phase of my life. I did not abandon my academic ambition. While working, I took the opportunity to sit for my GCE O' Level examination again, and by God's grace, I obtained good results.

However, my time in Ilorin was not entirely without challenges. I had some difficulties with one of my superiors because I resisted what I considered intimidation. At the same time, some aspects of my social life also created concerns. My father, who had always shown great concern for my welfare, quickly intervened. His intervention demonstrated once again the love and fatherly care he had for me.

Eventually, I was transferred to Jebba in 1984.

A New Beginning in Jebba

My arrival at Jebba marked the beginning of another important chapter in my life. I arrived with a renewed sense of focus, determination, and commitment to building my future.

My working experience at Jebba was particularly memorable. I enjoyed excellent cooperation with my senior officers, including Mr. Kingsley and Mr. Ajobiewe, as well as many other colleagues across different sections and departments.

Jebba also provided me with opportunities to serve God in whatever little capacity I could. Despite the demands of my work, I remained conscious of my relationship with God. Looking back, I can testify that God was faithful to me in every aspect of my life.

My service at Jebba was not merely about earning a living. It was a period in which I developed discipline, gained valuable experience, built relationships, and continued to pursue my dreams. The experiences I gathered during this period became part of the foundation upon which the later stages of my life were built.

From Jebba to Wushishi and Girkun

After my time at Jebba, I was transferred to Wushishi in Niger State. From Wushishi, I was later transferred to Girkun, close to Kutiwengi.

Each transfer introduced me to new people, new environments, and new responsibilities. Every station became another classroom of life. Through these movements, I learned how to adapt to changing circumstances, relate with different categories of people, and remain committed to my responsibilities regardless of where I found myself.

My Final Years With the Railway Corporation

From Girkun, I was transferred to Akerri in 1990. It was at Akerri that another major turning point occurred in my working life.

The retrenchment exercise that affected many workers of the Nigerian Railway Corporation eventually reached me. Like many other railway employees, I was compelled to leave the service. Thus, after years of commitment and dedication to the Corporation, my railway career came to an unexpected end through what was effectively a forced retirement.

Although the manner in which my service ended was not what I had anticipated, I chose not to allow the experience to destroy my hope or determination. Instead, I looked back with gratitude for the years I had spent with the Nigerian Railway Corporation.

Those years had taught me discipline, responsibility, courage, endurance, and the importance of continuous learning. They also gave me opportunities to meet people who influenced my life in different ways. Above all, they gave me countless reasons to acknowledge the faithfulness of God.

From my first day at the Traffic Training School in Ebute Meta to my final posting at Akerri, I experienced different phases of life—training, challenges, friendships, opportunities, dangers, disappointments, and divine protection.

When I look back today, I realise that my railway service was not simply a means of earning a living. It was one of the important instruments God used to prepare me for the next chapter of my life.

Preparation for My Marriage Life

As I look back on the different stages of my life and reflect on the experiences that shaped me, I have come to understand that every phase of life requires preparation. Life is not merely a collection of events that happen by chance; it is a journey in which God gradually leads a person toward the fulfilment of His purpose. As I approached the season of marriage, I began to understand that I needed more than the desire to get married. I needed to prepare myself for the responsibilities, commitments, challenges, and opportunities that marriage would bring.

Marriage, to me, was not simply about finding a wife and establishing a home. It was about entering into a lifelong relationship in which two people would share their journey, responsibilities, dreams, faith, challenges, and aspirations. I therefore needed to understand who I was, where I was going, what I wanted to achieve in life, and, most importantly, what I believed God wanted me to accomplish.

Understanding My Vision in Life

Vision is the clear picture of what a person desires to become, accomplish, or contribute during his or her lifetime. It gives direction to life and enables a person to make meaningful decisions instead of simply moving from one day to another without a clear sense of purpose.

As I approached marriage, I began to realize that my vision for life could not be separated from my vision for my family. I was not preparing merely to become a husband; I was preparing to become a partner, a provider, a father, and a responsible head of a home. I understood that the kind of family I wanted to build would be influenced greatly by the values, principles, and vision I carried into marriage.

One of the most important aspects of understanding one's vision is discovering one's purpose. Vision begins with the question: “Why am I here, and what has God called me to accomplish?”

The Bible says, “Where there is no vision, the people perish”

(Proverbs 29:18).

This Scripture became increasingly meaningful to me as I thought about my future. I realized that entering marriage without a sense of direction could create confusion and unnecessary struggles. A man needs to have some understanding of the kind of life he wants to build and the values upon which his family should be established.

My experiences had already taught me that life could be unpredictable. I had passed through difficult circumstances, experienced limitations, encountered disappointments, and met people whom God used at critical moments of my journey. These experiences helped me appreciate the importance of purpose and direction. I began to see that every stage of my life, including my preparation for marriage, was part of a much larger story that God was writing.

Discovering What I Could Contribute

God has given every person different gifts, talents, abilities, and opportunities. As I prepared for marriage, I had to consider not only what I wanted to receive from marriage but also what I could contribute to it.

Marriage requires much more than affection. It requires responsibility, sacrifice, patience, wisdom, commitment, understanding, and a willingness to grow together. I therefore began to understand that the abilities, knowledge, experiences, and opportunities God had given me were not intended solely for my personal advancement.

They could also become instruments through which I would serve, support, protect, and provide for my future family.

I knew that becoming a husband would require me to think beyond myself. I would have another person's welfare to consider, and eventually the lives and future of children would also depend on the decisions I made. This understanding made me take the preparation for marriage more seriously.

Preparing Myself to Become a Husband

As I approached marriage, I gradually realized that marriage was not simply about finding someone to love. It was also about becoming the kind of person who could love responsibly.

I had to prepare myself emotionally, spiritually, mentally, and practically.

I needed to understand that marriage would require me to make room for another person's dreams, weaknesses, expectations, aspirations, and opinions.

I would need to learn how to communicate, listen, forgive, encourage, compromise, and make decisions together. I would also need to learn how to disagree without destroying the relationship.

Above all, I came to understand that a successful marriage requires commitment beyond feelings. Feelings can change with circumstances, but commitment provides stability during difficult seasons. Love must therefore be expressed not only in words but also through sacrifice, patience, responsibility, faithfulness, and service.

The Kind of Home I Desired

As I reflected on my childhood and the experiences that had shaped me, I became increasingly conscious of the kind of home I wanted to establish.

I wanted my marriage to become more than a personal achievement. I desired a home where love, faith, discipline, respect, education, and godly values would have a strong foundation. I wanted to create an environment in which my wife would feel loved and valued and where my children would grow up knowing that they were wanted, cared for, disciplined, educated, and encouraged.

I desired to be able to provide adequately for my children and take responsibility for their welfare. I wanted to be present in their lives—not only as a provider but also as a father, teacher, counsellor, encourager, and example.

I wanted my children to learn from my life, both from my successes and from my mistakes. My desire was that they would have opportunities that I did not always have and that they would be able to pursue their own dreams while remaining grounded in godly principles.

I also understood that no marriage is perfect. Every couple must learn, adapt, forgive, communicate, and grow together. What matters is the willingness to remain committed to the covenant and to allow God to guide the family through both good and difficult seasons.

Looking Back at the Preparation

Today, when I look back at that period of my life, I can see that God was preparing me in ways I did not fully understand at the time. The earlier chapters of my life were not wasted experiences. The challenges, relationships, opportunities, disappointments, and moments of divine intervention all contributed to the person I was becoming.

My preparation for marriage therefore became another important chapter in the larger story of my life. I was moving from one phase into another—from personal development toward shared responsibility, from individual dreams toward family aspirations, and from preparing for my own future toward building a future with another person.

I came to understand that marriage is not the end of one's vision; rather, it can become part of the fulfilment of that vision. Conclusion

Understanding one's vision in life is about knowing where one is going, why one is going there, and what steps must be taken to get there. Vision gives meaning to experiences, discipline to decisions, and hope during difficult seasons.

As I prepared for marriage, I realized that I was not merely preparing for a wedding ceremony. I was preparing for a lifelong journey of companionship, responsibility, sacrifice, service, parenthood, and shared destiny.

The journey ahead would have its own challenges, but I had learned that challenges do not necessarily mean that the vision is wrong. Sometimes, they are part of the preparation for its fulfilment.

Looking back now, I can say that the hand of God was already guiding the different stages of my life. What I saw at the time as ordinary events were gradually becoming pieces of a larger picture. My preparation for marriage was therefore not an interruption of my life's vision; it was another significant step in the unfolding of that vision and the preparation for the family and future God had placed before me.

I return all glory back to God for His faithfulness despite all trials , troubles afflictions and challenges of life the marriage remain solidly stand firm by His mercy and abundant grace blessed with children and grandchildren, may His name be praise forever.

Anticipation for Divine Calling and the Wilderness Experience

Anticipation for divine calling is a significant stage in the spiritual journey of a believer. It refers to that period when a person gradually develops an awareness that God is preparing him or her for a particular assignment, ministry, responsibility, or purpose. It is a season when one begins to understand that life is more than the pursuit of personal ambitions; it is a journey through which God can lead an individual toward the fulfillment of His divine purpose.

In the journey of faith, divine calling does not always come as a sudden or dramatic experience. In many cases, God prepares His servants gradually through childhood experiences, family background, personal struggles, encounters with people, education, employment, opportunities to serve, disappointments, successes, and an inner desire to know and serve Him. Looking back, on my life I discovered that many events that appeared ordinary at the time were actually contributing to God's preparation for a future assignment.

An important aspect of divine calling is the wilderness experience.

The wilderness is often a place of preparation before public ministry and greater responsibility. It is a period when God separates an individual from certain familiar surroundings and uses solitude, trials, limitations, and difficult circumstances to shape character, deepen faith, and establish dependence upon Him.
